One summer afternoon, with golden light spilling like honey over the green grass of the Norwegian hills, I looked out into the distance and realised that the most precious thing studying abroad has given me was never a degree with distinction. Perhaps it is this: I fell in love with a whole world.

Not the romantic, glamorous love of the movies, and not love for any particular city. It was the feeling of stretching my comfort zone for the first time, of stepping out of my own cocoon and learning that out there are countless ways to live, countless people, countless skies and countless versions of myself waiting to be met.

So this is what studying abroad is: a first love with the world…

---

First loves always begin innocently. I loved the world before I had even met it. I loved it through photos of snow stretching white to the horizon, old European streets paved with stone, trains crossing pine forests, summer nights when the sun sets at ten. I loved it through other people's stories, through the words everyone passes along: "Go abroad, your life will be different."

Then one day I stood at the airport departure gate, hugging a suitcase of more than thirty kilos, packed with my family's love and my own ambitions. I didn't know then that the longest flight is not the one across continents. It is the one that slowly turns a child with family always at their back into an adult with their own life ahead.

---

The world out there is beautiful. But the world out there is also full of trials.

It doesn't know you were a top student. It doesn't care that you were your parents' pride, or notice how many prizes you once won. In a new country you are simply someone trying to find the right bus, someone standing for ages in front of a food counter because you can't read the labels, someone with Google Translate always open to ask a supermarket assistant the smallest thing, someone laughing a lot just to hide how lost you feel.

The world treats no one as special. But that is exactly why it treats everyone fairly.

---

Every first love has days when the heart breaks into pieces. Studying abroad is the same.

It is winter nights leaving work and class when it has been dark since four in the afternoon. It is a room with nothing but the sound of the heater and the wind against the window. It is the first time you fall ill and no one brings you a bowl of rice porridge, a birthday when you buy your own cake, a video call home that you end quickly, afraid your mother will see your red eyes.

Some kinds of homesickness don't necessarily make you want to go back, but they do make you understand that somewhere in the world there is a place where someone is always waiting for you.

And that, it turns out, is a kind of happiness too.

---

Then the world began to love me back, through the simplest things. An old woman smiling when I gave up my seat. A stranger helping me lift my suitcase onto the train. A colleague remembering that I like black coffee. A friend from a country half a world away who knows exactly the same feeling of missing home.

Little by little I came to see that people are alike everywhere: they get tired, they get lonely, they want to be listened to, they hope someone will call their name with all the tenderness in the world.

Borders can divide countries, but they have never divided what people truly feel.

---

And there are things you can only see clearly from very far away. I used to think home was perhaps just an address, a street, a corner. Now I know home is also the smell of fish sauce in a small kitchen, my mother calling me down to eat, a summer downpour in Hanoi, things so ordinary that while I was there I never truly knew how to treasure them.

Distance has a strange power. It doesn't make love smaller or thinner. It makes everything clearer.

---

But perhaps the most beautiful thing this love has given me is the chance to meet another version of myself. Someone who can go to the doctor alone, read a rental contract, earn money, spend money, cook a proper meal after ten hard hours of work, get back up after being turned down, smile gently at failure, and who knows that being strong doesn't mean never shedding a tear.

Being strong is waking up on time the next day, and going on living, going on hoping, going on loving life, even when life isn't always kind to you.

---

At some point I will leave this city, this country. Or maybe I'll stay, maybe I'll go home, maybe I'll fall for yet another country.

But I know there will always be a small corner on the map of the world where every road holds a piece of my youth. There is the first room I rented on my own, my first job, my first winter of falling snow, the first money I earned with my own work, the first time I sobbed from missing home.

And there, too, is the first time I realised I am brave enough to live anywhere in the world.

---

People often say a first love isn't meant to last a lifetime. It comes to teach you how to love.

Studying abroad is the same. What I take with me may not be just a degree or a nicer line on a CV. It is a more open heart, a more generous way of seeing difference, a deeper appreciation of the place where I was born and raised. And above all, it is a fierce belief that this world, hard as it is, has never been too small to hold someone trying to find their place in it.

---

If youth must have a first love, I am grateful that mine was not only with a person, but with the whole world.

A love that taught me that going very far, in the end, is not about becoming someone else.  
It is about coming back and, for the first time, truly understanding who I am.
